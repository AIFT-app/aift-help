# Your Account

Everything about you, rather than about a workspace, is on the **Account** page: your profile, your language and the emails you get. Open the account menu at the foot of the sidebar and click **Account**. The page has three tabs: **Profile**, **Language** and **Notifications**.

These settings are yours alone. They are not per workspace: change one once and it applies in every workspace and every organisation. No permission is needed, and nobody else can change them for you.

## Profile

### Profile picture

Your picture is shown next to your name across AI Finance Team, for example in the sidebar and in team lists. Without one, your initials are shown.

**Upload a picture** takes a PNG, JPG or WebP file up to 2 MB. A **Position your picture** window opens: drag the picture and use the **Zoom** slider until the circle shows what you want, with previews of how it will look on your profile, in a team list and in the sidebar. **Save picture** stores a square copy of at most 800 × 800 pixels, never the file you picked. Afterwards the buttons read **Replace** and **Remove**.

### Name

**First name** and **Last name** are how your name appears to others, and the first name is how emails greet you, so write it the way you like to be called. Under the fields the page previews both, for example *Display name: Anna Kovács · Greeting: Hi Anna,*. Each part can be up to 60 characters. In Hungarian the last name comes first, on this form and on the sign-up form, because that is the Hungarian convention.

### Job title

Up to 80 characters, shown next to your name to everyone you work with, for example *Senior bookkeeper*.

### Password

Type your **Current password**, then the **New password** twice. The new one must be at least 10 characters and different from the current one; a strength meter rates it as you type. A security check may appear before **Update password**. Once it is changed, AI Finance Team **signs you out on every other device** as a safety measure; you stay signed in where you are.

## Language

AI Finance Team is available in **English**, **Hungarian (Magyar)** and **German (Deutsch)**, and the interface is fully translated in all three: every screen, every button, every message.

Your choice is your own. It does not change what anyone else sees in the app. It does change one thing that reaches other people, and it is worth knowing before you switch: **a message you send a client is written in your language, not theirs.**

### Switching language

1. Open the account menu at the foot of the sidebar and click **Account**.
2. Open the **Language** tab.
3. Pick English, Magyar or Deutsch, then click **Save preferences**.

The interface switches straight away and you get a **"Language preference saved."** confirmation. The page itself puts it plainly: *"Choose the language for the AI Finance Team interface. Emails we send you will use the same language."*

One setting covers everything. It is not per workspace: change it once and it applies wherever you work, in every workspace and every organisation. No permission is needed, and nobody else can change it for you.

> If you have an old **Account → Preferences** link bookmarked, it still works. That page was split in two, and the language half now lives on the **Language** tab.

### What follows your language

- **The whole interface**: menus, labels, buttons, hints, error messages.
- **Emails the system sends you**: password resets, invitations, approval requests, document requests and message nudges are all written in the language of the person receiving them.
- **Numbers.** Hungarian groups thousands with a space and uses a decimal comma.
- **Month and period names**, so a report reads "July 2026" or "2026. július".

### What does not

This is the useful half of the article, because most of it surprises people.

#### Dates stay the same in every language

Every date reads **`2026-09-25`** and every timestamp **`2026-09-25 14:05`**, in English, Hungarian and German alike. This is deliberate: it is the form the accounting systems, NAV and bank statements all use, and it removes any doubt about which number is the day and which the month. Only month and period *names* follow your language.

#### Messages you send are in your language, not the reader's

The one place your setting reaches someone else. A notification the system generates is written in the recipient's language, but the email carrying a message you wrote is framed in **yours**, because that is the language you wrote the message in. A Hungarian accountant messaging a German-speaking client sends a Hungarian-framed email. Worth remembering when you choose who writes to whom.

#### Exports never follow your language

Not one of the four export files is affected by your setting:

| Export | Language of its headers |
|---|---|
| Ledger export | English, always |
| RLB posting file | Hungarian, fixed by the receiving software |
| HessynKettős file | Hungarian, fixed by the receiving software |
| VAT setup workbook | English sheet names always, plus a second header row in the **client's** country language |

The VAT workbook is the one to watch: its sheet names must stay English because the importer finds the sheets by name, and its second header row follows the workspace's country, not you. Importing back, each sheet may use any one of the three languages, but **mixing languages inside one sheet is rejected**, not guessed.

#### Text the AI wrote stays as the AI wrote it

Document summaries, triage reasoning, matching explanations and merge suggestions are stored as written and shown to you unchanged. The same goes for the type badge on a document that is not a contract: it prints the AI's own word, so a German chart of accounts reads `Kontenrahmen` on an English screen. See [Documents](/documents).

One AI feature is an exception and does follow you: **Ask the Ledger** answers in your language whatever language you type the question in.

#### Default transaction type names follow the client, not you

A workspace's built-in transaction types are shown in the **client's** country language, so a Dutch accountant keeping Hungarian books sees the Hungarian terms. That is on purpose: those names have to match what the client's own accountant and authorities use. Categories are not built in: a new workspace starts with none, and the office imports its own list. Anything you rename, or create yourself, is shown exactly as you typed it.

#### Legal documents fall back to English

Terms and privacy documents are only shown translated where a reviewed translation exists. Where one does not, you get the English master with a note saying so. Legal text is never machine-translated here.

### Before you sign in

You do not have to be signed in to read the app in your language. The sign-in, sign-up and password-reset pages carry a language picker, and so do the invitation, email-verification and legal pages. It is the three words **English · Magyar · Deutsch** in the corner of the card, with the current one in bold, rather than a dropdown.

With nobody signed in, the app picks a language in this order: a language in the link you followed, then the language of the invitation you were sent, then your browser's preference if it asks for Hungarian or German, then your country if you are in Hungary, Austria or Germany, and English otherwise. So **an English-language browser in Hungary gets a Hungarian page**, which is intended rather than a fault.

The whole order, signed in or not, is one ladder. The app takes the first rung that applies and stops there.

If you were invited to a workspace, the invitation arrives in the language the person inviting you chose, and your account is created already set to it. Nothing to change afterwards unless you want to.

### Your saved language follows you

Your language is saved on your account. **When you sign in, the app switches to it**, whatever language the sign-in page was in. That works the same on a new computer, after clearing your browser data, and on a computer someone else used before you: each person gets their own saved language when they sign in.

The language you pick on the sign-in page applies only until you sign in. To change it for good, use the **Language** tab.

## Email notifications

The **Notifications** tab has a switch for every email you can turn off. Everything starts switched on. A switch saves the moment you flip it; if saving fails, it flips back and says *"Could not save your preference; your setting was reverted."*

<AccountNotificationsFigure>
The top of the **Notifications** tab. The numbers match the list below.
</AccountNotificationsFigure>

1. **Monday summary**: what was handled and what is waiting, across all your workspaces.
2. **Product announcements**: occasional news about what shipped.
3. The two message emails. A single conversation can also be muted from the conversation itself.

| Section | Email | When it comes |
|---|---|---|
| **Weekly summary** | **Monday summary** | Mondays at 08:00, for accountants |
| **Product announcements** | **Product announcements** | occasionally |
| **Messages** | **Unread message reminders** | when a message to you stays unread; at most one per conversation per 10 minutes (see [Messages](/messages)) |
| | **Missing receipts** | when the books are missing a document from you; at most one a day, with a Monday summary (see [Missing Receipts](/documents-needed)) |
| **Approvals** | **Declined invoices** | for finance admins, when an approver declines an invoice |
| | **New items to approve** | once a wave of new items has settled |
| | **Daily digest** | on working days, at the time you choose (08:00 to start with) |
| | **Due-soon reminder** | on working days, at the time you choose (14:00 to start with), for invoices due within 2 days |

Every row is on everyone's page, but an email only goes to the people it is about: the Monday summary to accountants, the approval emails to approvers in companies where approvals are switched on. The two time pickers are in quarter hours, Budapest time. [Approving Invoices for Payment](/approving-invoices) describes the approval emails in full.

**Always sent**, at the bottom of the page, lists what cannot be turned off: **Security** (password and email changes, sign-in confirmations) and **Access** (workspace invitations, join requests and their decisions).

### Unsubscribing from an email

Every email you can turn off carries an unsubscribe link, and mail apps such as Gmail offer one-click unsubscribe from it. Neither needs you to sign in, and both turn off exactly the switch for that email; the page also offers to turn it back on. Do not be put off by its wording: the page always asks *"Turn off weekly summaries?"*, even when the link came from another email, but it turns off only the email you clicked it in. A link stops working after 180 days; use the **Notifications** tab instead.

# Working with Invoices

AI Finance Team reads every invoice for you: it extracts the data, works out which of your companies the invoice belongs to and whether it is income or an expense, finds the partner, and suggests categories and VAT codes. This article explains how invoices arrive, how to find and check them, and how to correct, archive and download them.

## How invoices arrive

- **Upload**: on the **Invoices** page, click **Upload invoices** in the top-right corner, or drop files anywhere on the page: PDF, JPEG, PNG, WebP or HEIC (iPhone photos, converted to JPEG before upload), up to 50 MB each, or a `.zip` archive, which the browser unpacks and queues file by file. A progress panel in the bottom-right corner follows each file. Once a file has uploaded, its extraction keeps going even if you leave or reload the page; a file that is still uploading from your browser is lost on reload. See [Uploading Files](/uploading-files).
- **Email**: forward invoices to the workspace's address. See [Forwarding Documents by Email](/email-forwarding).
- **NAV Online Számla** (Hungary): invoices reported to NAV arrive by themselves, without a PDF. See [NAV Online Számla](/nav-online-szamla).

### What happens next

Claude reads the invoice and extracts the header, the parties, the amounts and the line items. AI Finance Team then works out which of your companies is on the invoice: if your company is the seller, the invoice is income; if it is the buyer, it is an expense. It finds the partner, suggests a category for each line and the VAT codes, and writes a one-line summary. Categories are suggested once the direction is known. See [Categorising Invoices & Transactions](/categorization) and [VAT Codes on Invoices](/vat-codes-on-invoices).

### Duplicates

When you upload a file identical to one already in the workspace, AI Finance Team creates the invoice but marks it as a duplicate of the original and does not extract it again. The upload panel shows **Already exists**, and a click opens the original. After extraction, an invoice with the same invoice number, issue date, gross amount and currency as another one is marked a duplicate too.

Duplicates are hidden from the list: they appear as a **related** row you can expand under the original, and under **Record state: Duplicate**. If two invoices really are different (a supplier reused a PDF for two bills), open the duplicate and click **Mark as not a duplicate** on its banner.

## The invoice list

The invoice list with four invoices. The numbers match the list below. The companies and amounts are examples.
</InvoiceListFigure>

1. **Date**, with AI Finance Team's own ID underneath when your workspace shows IDs. Click the ID to copy it.
2. **Partner**, with the invoice number underneath. A long name wraps onto a second line. A click anywhere on the row opens the invoice.
3. **Labels**: what the invoice needs comes first, where it came from (**NAV**, **No document**) last. Up to three pills show, and **+N** stands for the rest: never a label that needs action, and pointing at it lists what it stands for.
4. **Amount**: the gross total in the invoice currency, green with an up arrow for income, red with a down arrow for an expense.

Click **Invoices** in the sidebar. The list has these columns: **Date** (the issue date) with the **ID** under it (AI Finance Team's own number, when your workspace shows it; click to copy), **Partner**, **Labels**, **Company** (only when your workspace has more than one company: which of your companies the invoice belongs to, or a dash if AI Finance Team has not assigned it yet) and **Amount**. It shows 50 invoices per page, sorted by ID, highest first, until you choose otherwise. Click any column header except **Labels** to sort by it: date, ID (the second line of the date header), partner, invoice number (the second line of the partner header), company or amount. The first click puts the date, ID and amount in descending order and the partner, invoice number and company in alphabetical order; the second click reverses it. Sorting by amount uses the amount converted into the base currency, so a foreign-currency invoice sits among invoices of the same value. An invoice that lacks the value you sort by (for example one with no company yet) comes last either way. An amber left edge marks an invoice that needs action. The list uses the full width of the window. In a narrow window it scrolls sideways, and the **Amount** column stays in view on the right.

### We expect an invoice document

Under **Settings → Ledger defaults → We expect an invoice document**, say whether this workspace expects a document (a PDF or an image) behind its invoices:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **No** | The default. No document is expected for any invoice |
| **For purchase invoices** | A document is expected behind purchase (supplier) invoices, not behind sales invoices |
| **For every invoice** | Behind purchase and sales invoices alike |

Where a document is expected, an invoice without one gets the **Document needed** to-do, and only there is the client asked for it on [Missing receipts](/documents-needed). Where none is expected, neither happens: the invoice keeps only the grey **No document** label.

When you accept on one invoice that no document will come (**No document expected**), the to-do leaves the invoice and the client is no longer asked for it: the request also disappears from [Missing receipts](/documents-needed). From then on the invoice carries the grey **No document expected** label.

Before saving, the dialog states how many invoices will have the to-do and how many open document requests to the client will close. When you narrow the setting, the requests that are already open close. When you widen it again later, the same requests reopen, and the client gets no second message about them. Changing the setting takes the **"Edit master data"** permission.

### Finding invoices

Search by invoice number, AI Finance Team ID, seller or buyer name, partner, tax number, amount, or a reference number printed on the invoice (order, delivery note, contract or project number, cost centre).

- **Accents are optional**: “szolgaltato” finds “Szolgáltató”.
- **Amount**: read as a Hungarian number, so “125000”, “125 000”, “125.000” and “125 000,00 Ft” are the same. It matches the gross, the net or the converted amount exactly, credit notes included.
- **Tax number**: in any form (12345678, 12345678-1-23, 12345678123).
- **Amount range**: two amounts with a spaced hyphen or two dots between them, such as “100 000 - 200 000” or “100000..200000”. The gross, net or converted amount must fall between them (both ends included), credit notes too. A hyphen without spaces (“2026-0012”) is searched as an invoice number.
- **Account number**: the supplier's account number as an IBAN or in domestic form, with or without spaces and hyphens (at least 12 digits).
- The matching text is highlighted. When the match is not visible in the row (the net amount or a tax number, for example), a **Match:** line under the partner says why the invoice is there.
- With no results, the list shows how many invoices the search finds without the filters and offers partners with a similar name.
- The **/** key jumps to the search. Empty, the search shows the last four searches on this list; they belong to your account, so they show on another computer too, and the next person on a shared computer does not see them. When a filter or a search is set, **Save the current filters** at the bottom of the field saves the whole setup (filters included) under a name; saved searches are at the top when the field opens, reopen with one click, and ✕ deletes them.

#### Search everything

The **Search everything** field at the top of the menu, under the workspace name (or ⌘K, Ctrl+K on Windows, or the magnifier in the phone header), searches the open workspace's partners, invoices, bank lines and pages at once, by the same rules as the list search. It also shows three more groups: **Invoice lines** (the text of an invoice's lines; a line opens on the **Lines & VAT** tab), **Documents** (attachments, contracts, other documents and uploaded bank statements, by file name, ID or account number) and **Messages** (the text of messages and comments on records; a message opens among the record's messages). Messages and lines are searched from three characters. A private message shows only to those who can see it on the record. When the exact search finds fewer than three invoices and bank lines, the field also searches **by meaning** a moment later (like the [Ledger](/ledger) Meaning mode) and shows similar items in a **By meaning (AI)** group, even when they do not use the words you typed. Only the search term is sent, to Voyage AI models running in the EU; only items with an AI summary can appear. If you have access to more than one workspace, you can choose under the field: **this workspace** or **all workspaces**. With all workspaces each result carries its workspace name and opens in that workspace, so you can see which client has a partner or an invoice number. Your browser remembers the choice. On the dashboard, where no workspace is open, it always searches all your workspaces. It shows up to five results per group; when there are more, the link at the bottom of the group opens the full list with the same term. Use the arrow keys to choose, Enter to open, ⌘/Ctrl+Enter to open in a new tab and Esc to close. Before you type it shows the last three records you opened from it. When the Invoices list finds nothing, its **Search everything** link searches the whole workspace for the same term.

The bar above the list filters by date range, **Company** (when you have more than one) and **Coding**, and the **Direction** switch picks **All**, **Income** or **Expense** in one click. The direction follows from the company, so invoices without one are listed under the **Company needed** to-do. With seven or more companies, the **Company** list has a search box. The other filters sit behind the **More filters** button: **Payment**, **Document**, **Partner**, **Source** (NAV or uploaded), **Record state**, **Processing**, and where approvals are switched on, **Workflow** and **Approver**. The button opens them from the right, and picking a value filters at once. The number on the button says how many of them are set, and every set filter shows under the bar, removable with its ✕. On a phone, **Company**, **Direction** and **Coding** move behind **More filters** too. Each filter offers every value of its own dimension and nothing from another one, and a step your workspace has switched off has no dropdown and no value. **Record state** opens on **Active**: archived invoices, duplicates and cancelled storno pairs stay hidden until you pick **Recently archived** (the last 30 days), **All archived**, **Excluded sales invoices**, **Duplicate** or **Cancelled**. **Excluded sales invoices** shows the uploaded sales invoices that a workspace processing only purchase invoices left out. Under **Payment**, **Overdue** lists unpaid invoices past their due date.

Where an approval is switched on, the **Workflow** filter groups its values by step: **Filing**, **Bookkeeping** and **Payment approval**. Within a group any of the values you pick will do (or); across groups every one must hold (and), and the filter bar spells the condition out, for example: Filing: approved AND Bookkeeping: approved. With at least two steps on, **Approved in every step that is on** picks the invoices approved in each of them in one click. **No approval needed** lists the invoices that need no payment approval; the invoice says why. With payment approval on, the **Approver** filter narrows to a person: a colleague who is the approver of at least one invoice, yourself (**Me**), or **No approver assigned**. Together with **Payment approval: Awaiting approval** it shows what waits on someone. Where the workspace names its accounting software, a **Handover** group joins them: **To hand over**, **Handed over**, **Changed after the handover** and, for RLB and Business Central, **Confirmed** (see [Ledger](/ledger)). **Bookkeeping: Approved for bookkeeping** together with **Handover: To hand over** lists what is approved and still to hand over.

The pills above the list are the to-dos. Each to-do pill uses the same words as the label it filters, and shows how many invoices carry that to-do under your other filters; a to-do with nothing to count has no pill, and a step your workspace has switched off has none either. If you work in a [focused period](/focused-period), the list follows it. A link saved before these filters existed still opens the same invoices. A selected to-do is marked blue with a ✕, and the row under the filter bar does not repeat it: that row shows only your other filters. When only to-dos are selected, **Clear filters** sits at the end of the to-do pills and clears the selected to-dos. **Collapse** at the end of the pills turns them into one **To do** button in the filter bar, with a number showing how many to-dos are selected. Hover over it, click it or open it with the keyboard to see the to-dos as a list with their counts; **Expand to-dos** at the bottom of the list brings the pills back. Your browser remembers the choice for you; by default the pills are shown.

With permission to edit invoices you can tick rows. The blue bar that appears offers **Re-categorize** and **Approve reviewed items**. Approval acts on the ticked invoices only: it clears the **Check the extracted data** and **VAT coded by default** flags and changes no amount or code.

When you pick the **VAT codes incomplete** to-do, **Re-run VAT coding** appears above the table, at the left of the pager. It runs on every invoice of the workspace with a missing VAT code, whatever the other filters, and fills only the missing codes. It skips invoices without a direction, because they need their company first (**Company needed**), so the confirmation may list fewer invoices than the to-do.

Some to-dos are right as they are: the extracted data was checked, a line total differs on purpose, no document will come, the default VAT code is correct, the invoice was handed over to the accounting software another way (**Handed over manually**, which needs the **"Run exports"** permission). The invoice's page then shows **Accept to-dos**: tick what is right, add a note if you like, and click **Accept**. The to-do goes, and the invoice keeps who accepted it, when, and the note. If the data changes afterwards, the to-do comes back. Where filing or bookkeeping approval is waiting on the invoice, that approval accepts these to-dos instead, and the **Approvals** card says which ones.

## Purchase tickets (felvásárlási jegy)

A felvásárlási jegy is the document a buyer issues when it purchases produce from a mezőgazdasági őstermelő, a private person under Hungary's agricultural special scheme. It is not an invoice: NAV Online Számla never carries it, so it arrives only by upload or email, and the 12% (plant products) or 7% (animal products) printed on it is not VAT but a compensation surcharge (kompenzációs felár), which the buyer deducts like input VAT.

AI Finance Team recognises it by the **FELVÁSÁRLÁSI JEGY** title and treats it as follows:

- The **purchase ticket icon** (a sheet with a leaf on it) marks it in the list before the partner name and on the invoice header before the number; point at the icon to read its name. There is no separate menu; it lives under Invoices.
- The issuing company is the **buyer** and the producer the **seller**, whatever the block on the document is called. The **Parties** card therefore reads **Producer** and **Purchaser**. In the buyer's own workspace the ticket is an expense.
- The producer becomes a real partner, identified by the 10-digit personal tax identifier (adóazonosító jel). The partner page shows it as a **personal tax id**, not as a malformed tax number, and the company register check does not run on it.
- The lines carry the 12% or 7% printed on the ticket. To receive a VAT code they need a 12% and a 7% rate element in the workspace's VAT master data (name them compensation surcharge), and a VAT code built on each. The rate printed on the ticket picks the line's rate element, not the AI. 12% is the surcharge on plant products, 7% on animal products. Until those exist, the tickets show the **VAT codes incomplete** label.
- When the ticket prints them, the **Overview** tab shows **Withheld income tax**, **Rounding** and **Amount payable** under the totals. The gross stays the ticket's printed gross.
- The producer's registration number and FELIR number are shown on the **Parties** card.
- The producer's personal data (social security number (TAJ), birth name, mother's name, place and date of birth, sex, citizenship and registration numbers) is read from the ticket by a separate step. It is stored on the partner, not on the invoice: the partner page shows it in the **Producer data** section, visible only to people with the dedicated permission. The payer needs it for the M sheet of its 08 return. It can be sent to RLB from the [Ledger's RLB page](/ledger).
- If the recognition was wrong, the **Document kind** field on the edit page switches between Invoice and Purchase ticket. This changes the icon only: correct the direction and the partner in their own fields.

## The invoice page

Click an invoice to open its page. The top card shows the invoice number, badges such as **Duplicate**, **Archived**, **Needs review** and **Completeness**, the payment status, and the facts **AIFT id** (AI Finance Team's own number for the invoice, always shown here; click to copy), **Partner**, **Direction**, **Gross** and **Issued**, with the **Edit** button and the **⋯** menu. Under it are the AI summary, which you can edit, and the **To-do** chips, for example a missing VAT code or a partner to verify.

The page has eight tabs:

- **Overview**: the **Parties** (seller and buyer, with your side marked), **Amounts** (net, VAT and gross, with the base-currency amounts where they differ) and **Dates** (issue, fulfilment, due, VAT and posting date), plus custom categories where they apply. When the invoice carries an order, delivery note, contract or project number or a cost centre, the **References** card shows it. These come from the NAV data or the uploaded document; where both have one, NAV's applies. The issuer's own general ledger account is not read. Above the payment approval card sits an **Approvals** card listing the checkpoints this invoice passes - filing, bookkeeping and payment - each with its state and who decided it. It appears even where only payment approvals are switched on. See [Filing and Bookkeeping Approval](/filing-and-bookkeeping-approval).
  - On the **Parties** card, clicking the partner's or your own company's tile (it shows **Details**) opens a side panel. For a partner you see the tax numbers, the bank accounts with their confirmation, the company register result and the rules set on it; for your company its identity, its bank accounts and the state of its NAV connection. The panel is read-only, so you can use it even if you cannot open the partner page. **Edit on the partner page** opens that page in the same tab, and its **Back to invoice** brings you back here, also after you switch tabs there. A partner that is not verified yet has no panel: its **Verify partner** button opens the partner picker instead.
- **Lines & VAT**: the line items with description and quantity, net, VAT rate, gross, category and VAT code. Where the workspace has [custom category lists](/custom-categories), each line's custom categories sit under the line and are set here.
- **Matching & payment**: the bank transactions matched to the invoice. See [Invoice Matching](/invoice-matching).
- **Document**: the original file, or the NAV rendering for a NAV invoice. The small icon right after the tab's name opens the same document in a separate window, from any tab, so it can stay open beside the invoice, for example on a second screen. The next invoice's document loads into that same window. An invoice that has no file yet, for example one that arrived from NAV, offers **Attach the invoice PDF** here: the file goes straight onto this invoice, nothing is extracted and nothing is re-filed. Afterwards **Run extraction** can read from the document what the NAV data lacks, such as the buyer's address, the bank account or the payment method; NAV's amounts, dates and tax numbers never change. This is the invoice's own document, not an attachment: supporting papers belong on the Attachments tab. If the file is wrong, for example an unreadable scan or another invoice's document, **Archive document** takes it off the invoice with a reason; you choose whether to archive only the document or also withdraw the data the AI read from it, and the dialog lists the fields that will be emptied before you confirm. NAV's data never changes. Under the viewer, **Archived documents** lists every file the invoice used to carry, with the reason, and **Restore** puts a file back as long as the invoice has no other document.
- **Attachments**: supporting documents such as a performance certificate, a timesheet or a delivery note, to preview, download or add. See [Invoice Attachments](/invoice-attachments).
- **Notes**: notes on the invoice, each visible only to you, to your side or to everyone. No email goes out. See [Invoice Notes](/invoice-notes).
- **Messages**: the conversation about the invoice. See [Messages](/messages).
- **History**: every change on a timeline, field by field with the old and the new value, who made it and when, together with extraction, matching and NAV events, the sends to Business Central and the HessynKettős exports. It can be filtered by person, and it cannot be edited.

Some things are corrected right on the page, such as the summary, the VAT and posting dates, the categories and the VAT codes.

## Editing an invoice

Click **Edit** on the invoice page, or press `E` when no field is focused. You need the permission to edit invoices. The editor opens full screen, with the document on the left (on wide screens) and the form on the right:

- **Assignment**: whether you are the seller or the buyer (**We are the seller · income** or **We are the buyer · expense**), which of your companies it is, and the partner. The names and tax numbers printed on the invoice are not edited: you choose who the invoice belongs to.
- **Invoice details**: the invoice number, the currency, and the issue, due, fulfilment, VAT and posting dates.
- The net, VAT and gross totals.
- **Line items**: the description, net, gross and category of each line can be edited; the VAT rate is shown but not edited. You pick the category from a searchable list: type a name or a code to filter. Categories are listed by code, ascending, and the AI's suggestion, if there is one, sits at the top. On a line that already has a category, clicking the field opens an empty search box, and the current category is ticked in the list; type to search. The category changes only when you pick a row, and closing the list shows it in the field again. To remove it, pick **- No category -** at the top of the list. **Add row** adds a line, and the bin icon removes one.

Click a value to edit it; it is saved when you press Enter or click away. There is no Save button. When you hover over a field, the value is highlighted in yellow in the PDF, so you can check it against the source. A field you changed shows a revert arrow on hover, which restores the value the AI extracted. Press `Esc` or click **×** to close the editor.

## Archiving an invoice

An invoice that should not be in the books, because it is private, was sent to you by mistake or belongs to none of your companies, is **archived**, never deleted. Open the **⋯** menu in the top card of the invoice, click **Archive invoice** and give a reason of at least three characters: who archived it, when and why stay on the invoice. Before you confirm, the dialog lists what archiving will do to this invoice: a confirmed bank match is unlinked and the transaction returns to its own review, pending match suggestions are discarded, a line already on a payment file is withdrawn, the invoice leaves its settlement session, an open request to the client is closed, and the dialog warns when the invoice has already been pushed to Business Central or has an intercompany twin, which you then settle by hand.

If the reason is that the invoice belongs to none of your companies, the company choice on the invoice offers the shortcut **Archive: no company of ours**. It opens the same dialog with the reason filled in and clears the company.

An archived invoice carries a grey **Archived** badge, and its banner shows who archived it, when and why, with a **Restore** button. Until it is restored it stays outside every list, count, review, approval queue and export. Restoring brings back the invoice and nothing else: a bank match that was unlinked is not re-created; if it was right, match it again by hand.

Archiving an invoice whose bookkeeping approval has already been given also needs the permission to approve bookkeeping; the dialog says so, and the button stays disabled without it.

## Downloading an invoice

On the invoice page, open the **⋯** menu in the top card and choose a format under **Download**:

| Format | Contents |
|--------|----------|
| **PDF** | The original document |
| **Excel (XLSX)** | Two sheets: "Invoice" with the summary and "Line Items" with every line |
| **CSV** | The invoice header on the first rows, then the line items |
| **JSON** | All invoice fields and a `line_items` list |

The files are named after the original file or, without one, the invoice number. XLSX, CSV and JSON are built from the current data, so they include your corrections; their column headers are in English. **PDF** is in the menu only when the invoice has a file. XLSX, CSV and JSON are available on every invoice, including those from NAV. You can also download the PDF with **Download** on the **Document** tab.

## Downloading several invoices at once

You can download the documents of a period, or of the invoices you ticked, as one ZIP file, with a CSV file holding the invoices' data next to them. The ZIP is built in your browser: the files come straight from storage, nothing runs in the background, and the download changes no invoice and no setting.

### Where it starts

<DocumentExportEntryFigure>
The header of the Invoices page and the selection bar with three rows ticked. The numbers match the list below.
</DocumentExportEntryFigure>

1. **Download** in the page header, next to **Upload invoices**: downloads the invoices of a period. The dialog opens with the period and the date basis of the list's date filter; when the list has no period picked, it says **All time**, so pick a month or a quarter. Everyone who can see invoices has this button, and it downloads exactly the invoices your list shows: someone who sees only their own invoices gets their own.
2. **Download** in the selection bar: downloads the ticked rows, whatever the period and the other filters. Rows can be ticked by people who can edit invoices. An archived invoice or one not to be booked stays out even when ticked; the dialog says how many.
3. The ticked rows. One ZIP holds at most 2,000 invoices, a selection included.

The download holds the same invoices as the [Ledger](/ledger) export for the same period and date basis: no duplicates, no rejected, archived or not-to-be-booked invoices.

### The Download invoices dialog

<DocumentExportDialogFigure>
The Download invoices dialog before an October download, with the Advanced section open. The numbers match the list below.
</DocumentExportDialogFigure>

1. **Period** and its date basis: **Issue date**, **Fulfillment date**, **Due date**, **VAT date** or **Posting date**, the same choice as on the list and in the Ledger.
2. **Direction**: **All**, **Incoming** or **Outgoing**. In a workspace with more than one company, **Company** beside it narrows the download to one company.
3. **Format**: **ZIP (documents + CSV)** (the default), **CSV only** (only the invoices' data, no documents), and in a German or Austrian workspace **DATEV (Belegtransfer)**, see below.
4. **File name pattern**: the buttons under the field (**Fields for the file name**) insert a field at the cursor, for example **Invoice number**, **Issue date** or **Partner**, and you can type free text between the fields. Under them, **Files will be named like this (first three):** shows the first three real file names, made by the same rule the download uses.
5. **Folders in the ZIP**: **None** (the default), **By month** (the month of the chosen date basis, for example `2026-10/`), **By direction** (`Incoming/`, `Outgoing/`) or **By partner**.
6. **Advanced**: **File names without accents** and **CSV format**, see below.
7. The summary follows the filters: how many invoices, how many documents, how large, and how many invoices have no document (NAV data only).
8. **Download**: starts the download, and the dialog closes.

The dialog opens with the settings of your last download in this workspace: format, pattern, folders, accents, CSV format and the DATEV folder names, so a monthly routine is one click. The period comes from the list; direction and company always start at **All** and **All companies**.

### File names

<DocumentExportNamingFigure>
The file name of one invoice under five different patterns. The highlighted part is what a rule changed.
</DocumentExportNamingFigure>

The default pattern is `{issue_date}_{partner_name}_{invoice_number}`, for example `2026-10-03_Reamwell Office Supplies Ltd._RW-2026-00418.pdf`. These rules apply to the names a pattern makes:

- Characters a file name cannot hold (`/ \ : * ? " < > |`) become hyphens: the invoice number `SBR/2026/0342` becomes `SBR-2026-0342`.
- An empty field is dropped together with one separator next to it. If nothing is left, the file is named by its AIFT ID.
- If two files would get the same name (upper and lower case count as the same, as on Windows and macOS), the later one gets `_` and its AIFT ID appended, for example `2026-10-03_Reamwell Office Supplies Ltd._RW-2026-00418_DEMO-INV-2026-0256.pdf`.
- **File names without accents** replaces accented letters with plain ones (`ő` → `o`, `ß` → `ss`), for programs that handle them badly.
- A name has at most 120 characters before the extension; if it would be longer, the partner name is shortened first.
- A PDF that holds several invoices goes into the ZIP once per invoice, under each invoice's own name.

### What is in the ZIP

<DocumentExportZipFigure>
The same two-month download without folders and with a folder per month, and three rows of its CSV. The Slatebridge invoice failed to download and the Gearmont invoice is NAV data only, so neither is in the ZIP.
</DocumentExportZipFigure>

The downloaded file is named after the workspace and the period, for example `Pelda-Kft_2026-10-01_2026-10-31_invoices.zip` (for ticked invoices, their earliest and latest date). The ZIP always has `invoices.csv` at its root.

The CSV has **one row per invoice**, including the ones without a document. The column order is fixed and the headers are in the app's language. The first column is **File name in ZIP** (empty when the invoice is not in the ZIP), the second **Document**: **Included**, **No document** or **Download failed** (in a DATEV package also **DATEV: zero amount** or **DATEV: file too large**). Then the AIFT ID, the invoice number, the direction, the five dates, the partner and its tax ID, the company, net, VAT and gross in the invoice's currency and in the base currency, the payment and the matching status, the source (NAV, upload, email), the original file name and a link to the invoice's page. Dates are YYYY-MM-DD and the file is UTF-8.

**CSV format** has two options: **Semicolon and decimal comma (for Hungarian and German spreadsheet settings)**, the default when the app is in Hungarian or German, and **Comma and decimal point (standard)**, the default in English.

**Why is an invoice in the CSV but not in the ZIP?** For invoices that came from NAV Online Számla, NAV provides data, not a PDF, so there is nothing to put in the ZIP, and no stand-in document is generated. They are in the CSV marked **No document**, and the dialog's summary says beforehand how many there are. If you upload the PDF later, download the period again.

### Limits

One download holds at most **2,000 invoices** and **500 MB** of documents. Above that, the dialog says so, **Download** stays disabled, and you pick a shorter period. A DATEV package is tighter: at most **4,999 documents** and **450 MB**, and a file over 20 MB is left out (**DATEV: file too large**).

### While it runs

<DocumentExportToastsFigure>
The download's notification while it runs, and at the end when two files failed.
</DocumentExportToastsFigure>

After **Download** the dialog closes and a notification in the bottom-right corner shows the progress: **Downloading invoices: 123 / 233**. You can keep working anywhere in the workspace; if you close or reload the browser tab, the browser asks first, because the ZIP is being built in that tab. **Cancel** stops the download and saves nothing. One download runs at a time.

At the end the browser saves the ZIP and the notification says how many documents are in it. Each file is tried three times; if it still fails, the notification says how many failed and the CSV marks them **Download failed**. Download those again, for example by ticking them.

### DATEV (Germany, Austria)

<DocumentExportDatevFigure>
The dialog with the DATEV format, and what the DATEV package holds. The numbers match the list below. This format is offered only in German or Austrian workspaces.
</DocumentExportDatevFigure>

1. **DATEV (Belegtransfer)** is offered only in workspaces whose country is Germany or Austria. It builds a DATEV XML-Schnittstelle online package, which you upload to DATEV Unternehmen online with the DATEV Belegtransfer program: hand it the ZIP without unpacking it.
2. The file names follow your pattern, but hold only unaccented letters, digits, dots, hyphens and underscores, with `_` for spaces. The package has no folders, so **Folders in the ZIP** is not shown.
3. **DATEV folder: incoming invoices** and **DATEV folder: outgoing invoices**: the folder in DATEV the documents go to, `Eingangsrechnungen` and `Ausgangsrechnungen` by default. Change them if yours have other names in DATEV.

The package holds `document.xml` and, per invoice, a PDF and an XML file of the same name, from which DATEV makes the document's entry. No account numbers are sent: the account assignment happens in DATEV. **Downloading a month again never creates duplicates**: every invoice goes into the package under its own ID, and DATEV skips a document it already has, so a PDF that arrived late can be sent by downloading the month again. NAV-only invoices without a document stay out here too, and so does an invoice with a zero amount (**DATEV: zero amount**).

The CSV is not in the DATEV package, because DATEV would not know what to do with it. At the end of the download, the notification's **Download CSV** button gives you the CSV of the same invoices as a separate file.

## Extraction and review

The upload panel shows each file as **Queued**, **Uploading…**, **Extracting…**, **Extracted** or **Failed**, with **Retry** for a failed one. While an invoice is still being read, its page shows the extraction state in the top card.

The **Review** label means the extraction scored below 80% for completeness: the invoice number, a date, a tax number, an amount or the line items are missing, or the totals do not add up. The invoice page shows the score as **Completeness: N%**. A failed extraction is labelled **Review** too. Check such invoices against the document and correct them in the editor.

## Who can do what

Seeing the workspace's invoices needs the permission to view invoices and bank transactions. A member without it still sees the invoices they uploaded themselves, and any invoice routed to them for approval: six tabs instead of eight, no labels, no payment status and no to-dos, with a note on the list saying only their own invoices are shown.

Editing, archiving and restoring, marking or unmarking a duplicate, and the bulk actions all need the permission to edit invoices, which accountants hold by default (archiving an invoice whose bookkeeping approval is already given also needs the permission to approve bookkeeping); client users can view invoices, open the document and upload. Everyone can download the invoices they see with **Download** in the page header; downloading ticked invoices, like any selection, needs the permission to edit invoices. See [Roles and Permissions](/roles-and-permissions).

## Tips

- **Suppliers and customers are partners.** If a supplier is not recognised, add or verify it under Master data, Partners. **Companies** are your own businesses. See [Managing Partners](/partners).
- **Use the History tab** to see who changed what and when.
- **Download as XLSX** to share an invoice with a colleague who works in Excel: the summary and the lines stay on separate sheets.

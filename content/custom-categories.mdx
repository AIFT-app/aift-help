# Custom Categories

Beyond the built-in Invoice and Cash categories, every workspace can define its own **custom category lists**: extra ways of labelling invoices and bank transactions, such as cost centers, company cars, or funding sources. The AI applies them automatically in a separate pass, and anything it cannot decide on a mandatory list is flagged for manual review.

You'll find them under **Master data → Custom Categories**: one tile per list, and each tile opens the list's own page.

## Creating a List

A list has:

- **Title**: what accountants see everywhere ("Cost centers").
- **Description**: plain-language guidance the AI reads word for word when it categorizes. Write it the way you would brief a junior colleague: what the list means, how to choose, and which category is the catch-all.
- **Applies to**: invoices, bank transactions, or both.
- **Mandatory**: when the AI cannot assign a category with enough confidence, the item is flagged for review, just like other problems.
- **Active**: inactive lists are not applied to new items and are not sent to the AI.

## Categories

Each list holds categories with an optional **Code** (your own identifier, e.g. `CC-100`), a **Name**, and an optional **Description** that becomes part of the AI's vocabulary. You can add them one by one or import them from Excel with the same flexible import used for the built-in categories: upload a file, confirm the column mapping, review what would be created, and nothing existing is ever overwritten.

## Building a List from a Document

If the categories already exist in a document, you do not have to type them in. Open the document under **Documents**, click **Create category list**, and the AI reads it and proposes a list: a title, the guidance text, and the categories with their codes and descriptions.

It works on any document you have uploaded, up to twenty pages: a costing manual, a chart of accounts, a department list, a grant budget. Nothing happens on its own; the AI reads a document only when you ask it to.

What comes back is a **proposal**, and you are the one who decides:

- Every field is editable before anything is created, including the list title and the guidance text.
- Individual categories can be excluded with **Exclude**.
- A category the document marks as closed or retired is created as inactive, and says so before you commit.
- You can send the categories into a **new list**, or add them to an **existing list**, in which case only what is missing is added and nothing already there is touched.

The last step shows exactly what will happen: how many categories will be created, how many are already there, and how many rows could not be used. Nothing is written until you click **Create**.

If the document holds no categorisation scheme, the screen says so plainly rather than offering an empty list. That is a normal answer for most documents.

A list built this way remembers where it came from, and the document shows which lists it produced, so the origin of a set of codes is never a mystery later.

## How the AI Applies Them

After an invoice or transaction finishes its normal categorization, a separate AI pass reads your lists and assigns one category per applicable list. Confident assignments are applied automatically and shown with an AI badge and confidence; uncertain ones are left empty. On a **mandatory** list, an empty assignment flags the item:

- invoices show an amber to-do chip and count into the monthly closing checks;
- bank transactions show the chip on their detail page, and the transaction list has a **Custom category missing** filter.

A category you pick by hand always wins; the AI never overwrites an existing assignment, whether it came from you or from an earlier run.

## Covering Existing Items

A new list only applies to items that arrive after it. To cover history, open the list and use **Apply to existing items**: the AI processes up to 2,000 older invoices and transactions per run, and you can run it again for the rest.

## Using a List in Reports

A list is not only a label. On any report you can pick it under **Break down by**, and every reporting line splits into one column per category, so a single P&L also shows how the numbers divide across cost centres, business lines or projects.

Only lists that match the report are offered: an Invoice report offers lists that apply to invoices, a Cash report those that apply to bank transactions. The report also shows what carries no category yet, and what share of the period is covered, so a split is never more confident than the underlying data. See [Reports](/reports) for how to read it.

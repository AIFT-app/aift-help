# Ledger

The Ledger is where you answer a question with your data: every expense in EUR last quarter, every transaction on one bank account in March, every invoice from one supplier this year. It browses your invoices and bank transactions together, filters them, and exports the result.

It lives under **Exports → Ledger** in the sidebar, and clicking **Exports** lands you here directly. Client-side users never see it.

Opening it needs the **"Run exports"** permission. That is a permission rather than a job title, so an accountant can be without it, and the sidebar still shows the link: pressing it returns you to the workspace overview with no message. If that happens to you, that is the permission you are missing.

## What you are looking at when it opens

Two defaults decide what you see first, and neither is obvious:

- The date range starts at **the last 30 days**.
- Invoices are measured on the workspace's default date type, which is **Fulfillment date** unless someone changed it under **Settings**.

So a Ledger that looks nearly empty on a busy month usually is not: widen the range.

## The five tabs

| Tab | One row per | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **Invoice Headers** | invoice | totals, dates, company, partner, direction |
| **Invoice Lines** | line item | the same invoices, down to each line, with category and VAT detail |
| **Bank Transactions** | bank movement | what actually reached the account |
| **Companies** | company | the workspace's companies, read only |
| **Partners** | partner | the workspace's verified partners, read only |

The first three are the working tabs. **Companies** and **Partners** are registry lists: no filter strip, no search bar, and their rows do not open anything. On the three working tabs a row is a link, to the invoice or to the transaction.

Duplicates and rejected invoices never appear, on any tab or in any export.

### A filter does not mean the same thing on every tab

The article this replaces promised that one filter answers the same question from both sides. It does not, and the differences matter:

- **Category filters are ignored on Invoice Headers**, by design, because a category belongs to a line and not to an invoice. The app says so in an amber notice, **"Category filters don't apply here"**, with a **Show invoice lines instead** link. Follow it.
- Bank-only filters (transaction type, bank account, cash-flow category) do nothing to invoice rows, and invoice-only filters do nothing to bank rows.
- The date type is an invoice idea. Bank transactions are always measured on their booking date.

What genuinely crosses all three: the date range, direction, company, partner and currency.

## Filtering

### Ask the Ledger

The bar at the top is labelled **Ask the Ledger**. Type an ordinary sentence and the app turns it into filters. It then shows **"Understood as:"** with its reading of your request and an **Undo** if it got it wrong.

Ask in any language you like: the answer comes back in your interface language either way.

Beside the bar is a **Search engine** choice: **Auto**, **Exact** or **Meaning**. This is worth knowing, because Meaning is a different page rather than a different filter. It searches the AI's summary of each record, so only records that have one appear, and **filters, sorting and export do not apply there** - the Export button is simply absent. On **Auto** a vague question can route you there by itself, which is why a search for something like "travel costs" can land you on a page with no Export button.

When a search returns nothing or almost nothing, a banner offers to look by meaning instead: *"N records match by meaning."* with a **Show them** button.

### The filter strip

There is no "Show filters" button. The common controls are always on screen, in a strip under the search bar: the **date range** with its date-type dropdown, **Direction**, and either a partner picker or, on the bank tab, a bank account. Everything else is behind **More filters** on the right, which carries a count of how many you have set.

Whatever you apply appears as a row of chips under **Active filters**. Click a chip's × to drop one, or **Reset all** to clear them. When nothing matches, the app says *"No results match these filters."*

**Date filtering is strict.** An invoice that has no date of the type you picked is left out rather than falling back to another date, and the strip counts them for you so they are not invisible.

Everything is in the address bar: the tab, the filters, the sort, the page. A Ledger view can be bookmarked or pasted to a colleague.

## Exporting

Click **Export** and pick a format first, then a scope:

| | **Export all three sheets** | **Export this view only** |
|---|---|---|
| **Excel workbook (.xlsx)** | one workbook, every dataset | one workbook, the tab you are on |
| **CSV bundle (.zip)** | a zip, one CSV per dataset | a zip with one CSV |

**Despite its label, "all three sheets" produces five**: invoice headers, invoice lines, bank transactions, partners and companies. The label was written when there were three and has not caught up.

The two master-data sheets are the workspace's full registries. Your filters shape the three transactional sheets only.

Two limits and one silent subtraction:

- Above **50,000 rows** you are asked to confirm.
- Above **200,000 rows** the export is refused until you narrow it, with a chip reading *"Refine filters to export - over 200000 rows."*
- **Invoices whose VAT coding is incomplete are left out of the export.** Afterwards a summary tells you how many, with a **Review excluded** link that filters the headers tab down to exactly those invoices. Check this before reconciling a row count against anything. [VAT in exports](/vat-in-exports) explains the gate.

The file is named for the period you exported.

## Tips

- **Widen the date range first.** Most "where is my data" moments are the 30-day default.
- **Ask the Ledger for the first cut, the chips for the fine-tuning.** The sentence gets you to the right ballpark; the chips are exact.
- **Reconciling a total? Read the excluded-invoice summary**, or the export will be quietly short.
- **Sending a view to a colleague?** Copy the address rather than describing the filters.

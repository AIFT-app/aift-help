# Forwarding Documents by Email

Every workspace has its own email address. Forward an invoice or a receipt to it and it appears in the workspace within about a minute, with no logging in and no manual upload. It is the easiest way to capture the SaaS receipts and supplier invoices that already arrive in your inbox.

Forwarded email lands in **Intake**, the log of everything received at your workspace addresses. Reading and classifying the attachments takes longer than the minute: that is the email showing up, not the invoice being finished.

## Your Address

Open **Settings → Email ingestion**, or **Manage addresses →** at the top of the Intake page. The **Primary address** is a copyable block that looks like this:

```
in-k7p2xq4m@in.aifinance.team
```

The part before the `@` is eight random characters, not your company name. That is deliberate, and the next section explains why. The page also shows how many emails arrived in the last 30 days and when the last one was.

The address is **per workspace**, not per company or per person, and it cannot be renamed, changed or regenerated.

### Who can send to it

**Anyone who knows the address.** There is no list of permitted senders. The only check is that the sending domain passes its own authentication, which proves the mail really came from that domain, not that the person is anyone in particular.

So the address is the secret. That is why it is eight random characters rather than your company name: nobody can guess it, and nobody can work out your other workspaces' addresses from one of them. Treat it the way you would treat a private upload link. Share it with the people and the billing systems that should be sending you documents, and not more widely.

### Custom aliases

If a supplier's billing page needs a tidier address, you can claim up to **five custom aliases** per workspace, for example `acme@in.aifinance.team`. Under **Custom aliases**, type the name, click **Claim**, and use it anywhere the primary address works.

**A custom alias is guessable, and that is the trade.** Choosing your own company's name gives up the protection the random address provides. Worth it for a supplier portal that rejects odd-looking addresses, not worth it as your everyday address.

**Retire** switches an alias off, permanently. Mail to a retired alias is rejected rather than accepted, and the name is reserved for good: no other workspace can ever claim it, and neither can you. Retiring one does free a slot for a different name.

Claiming and retiring need the "Manage connections" permission, which accountant admins and finance leads hold by default. Everyone else sees the addresses read-only, which is all you need in order to forward.

## What You Can Send

Accepted attachments: **PDF, PNG, JPEG, WEBP and HEIC**. ZIP archives are opened and their supported contents ingested as separate documents.

**Everything else is dropped silently.** A `.docx`, `.xlsx`, `.csv` or `.txt` attachment is discarded with no warning, and the email still shows as completed with a lower attachment count. The count you see is what was kept, not what was sent, so if a number looks low, something was dropped.

Limits: 10 MB per attachment, 20 attachments per email, 25 MB for the whole email, and 200 emails per workspace per day.

Two things that catch people out:

- **An invoice pasted into the email body is lost.** The body is stored and you can read it on the email, but it is never treated as a document. Only attachments are.
- **Images under 10 KB are dropped as signatures and logos.** A small screenshot of a receipt can disappear this way. Larger images embedded in the message are ingested like any attachment.

> **iPhone photos need care.** A HEIC photo is accepted by the mailbox and then fails when it is read, leaving the document as **Failed** and the email stuck on **Processing**. Send the original PDF where you can, or set your phone to take photos in JPEG.

Forwarding the same file twice costs nothing: the second copy is recognised by its contents, marked as a duplicate and never processed again.

## What Happens Next

1. The email arrives and a row appears in **Intake**.
2. Each attachment is classified: invoice, receipt, credit note, bank statement, contract, or something else.
3. **Invoices, receipts and credit notes** go into extraction and appear on the **Invoices** page like any other upload.
4. **A bank statement is not extracted this way.** It waits in Intake marked **Awaiting extractor**, and it appears in no queue and carries no badge, so nothing will remind you. Upload it on the **Bank transactions** page instead: see [Uploading Bank Statements](/bank-statement-upload).
5. **A contract or anything unrecognised** goes to the **Documents** page, which is accountant-side only. It does not appear under Needs review.
6. **Anything classified with low confidence** is flagged under **Needs review**, whether or not it also went on to extraction.

Nothing is ever sent back to the sender: no reply, no bounce, no confirmation, for success or failure. The only way to know an email arrived is to look at Intake.

The email itself is never billed to you as a document; only the attachments that become invoices count.

The app also learns from who sends what. Once a sender's domain has produced a few confident classifications, later documents from that domain are classified better, so the tenth invoice from a supplier lands more reliably than the first.

## The Intake Page

Intake has two tabs:

- **All mail** - every email received, newest first, with when it arrived, who sent it, the subject, which of your addresses it was sent to, a status, and how many attachments were kept.
- **Needs review** - the subset needing a human decision, with an amber count badge.

Seeing any of it needs the "View invoices and bank transactions" permission. A member without it sees an empty Intake rather than an error. The addresses themselves stay visible to everyone.

### Status labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Completed** | Processed and its attachments handled |
| **Processing** | Still being read |
| **Failed** | Something went wrong handling the email |
| **Retired address** | Sent to an alias that was retired |
| **Auth failed** | The sending domain could not be verified |
| **Too large** | Over the 25 MB limit |
| **Rate limited** | More than 200 emails in a day |
| **Trial limit reached** | The mail was fine; the trial plan's document allowance refused it |
| **Multi-document** | One PDF held several documents |
| **No attachments** | Nothing supported to ingest |

**No attachments** is worth reading carefully: it means no *supported* attachments. An email carrying only a Word file shows up this way.

**A typo in the address leaves no trace at all.** Mail to an address that does not exist is dropped without a row, without a bounce and without an error. If you forwarded something and Intake is empty, check the address first.

## When Something Needs Attention

Open an email's row to see its attachments and the body.

- **An attachment could not be saved.** The count turns amber with a banner saying so. Upload that file manually from the Invoices page. Accountants can also download the raw email to see exactly what arrived.
- **A PDF held several documents.** It is flagged **Multi-document** and nothing is extracted from it. Split it into one document per invoice and forward the pieces.
- **The app was not sure what a file was.** It lands under **Needs review** with its best guess. Open it and use the reclassify control to set the right type: **Invoice**, **Bank statement**, **Receipt**, **Credit note**, **Contract** or **Other**. Any member can do this, and correcting it teaches the app for that sender's domain.

## Tips

- **Forward the original.** The supplier's PDF extracts far better than a photo of a screen, and it avoids the HEIC problem entirely.
- **One invoice per email is ideal**, but several supported attachments on one email are fine: each becomes its own document.
- **Check the attachment count** after forwarding something unusual. It is the quickest way to notice that a file was silently dropped.
- **Keep the primary address semi-private**, and claim a custom alias only where a supplier's system forces you to.

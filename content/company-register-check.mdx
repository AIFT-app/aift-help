# Checking Suppliers Against the Company Register

AI Finance Team can check your suppliers against the Hungarian company register. It tells you whether the bank account on a supplier invoice is the one the supplier reported to the register, fills in partner details you are missing, and lists anything that differs so you can look at it.

The check is switched on per workspace, and off until we switch it on. If you do not see it, ask your AI Finance Team contact.

## The Four Answers

Most partners start in the first state, and a workspace that has just been switched on will be full of it.

- **Not checked yet.** Nothing has asked the register about this partner. A check runs when a new supplier invoice arrives.
- **Registered.** The register lists this company, and, where an account was compared, lists that account for its tax number. The line says since when, and when it was checked.
- **Not registered.** The register does not list this account, or this company, for that tax number. **This is not proof of fraud.** A bank has eight days to report a newly opened account, so a recent account may be perfectly genuine. Foreign companies with a Hungarian tax number and organisations that are not companies are not listed there either. Check with the supplier, on a number you already know, before paying.
- **Cannot be checked.** We could not ask, or the register could not answer. The reason is always shown: no tax number, a foreign supplier, a VAT group identifier rather than a company's own number, the partner used for private-person sales, this month's lookups are used up, or the register subscription refused. **Cannot be checked is never a negative result.**

On a partner with no bank account on file, the section says **Found in the register** instead: the lookup worked, there was just no account to compare.

## Where You See It

- **On a supplier invoice**, on the payment approval card, under the payee account.
- **On the payment file page**, on each candidate line.
- **On a verified partner**, in the **Company register** section: what the register says, what it filled in, the differences, and a **Company details** block with the legal form, registration number, main activity, and the figures from the last filed report.
- **In the verify dialog**, when you turn a counterparty into a partner: if the register knows the company, its legal name, tax number, registered address and main activity are offered, and **Use these details** fills them in.

## What Starts a Check

- **A supplier invoice from a partner nobody has checked yet.** Only expense invoices, and only suppliers: a customer is never sent to the register.
- **You, with Check now** on a partner that has no result yet, or **Revalidate** afterwards.
- **A changed payee account**, which always forces a fresh check, because that is exactly the case the control exists for.
- **Editing the partner**, which re-checks from data we already hold, at no cost.

Invoices that arrived before the check was switched on are not checked. If a client needs their history covered, ask us: we can run a capped backfill that walks the existing suppliers.

### What a check costs, and what it does not

Each check uses one of the month's register lookups. **Searching the register by name is free**, and so is a re-check that only recomputes from data already fetched. Revalidating the same partner twice within an hour is refused.

The register's own data is refreshed at most every 90 days. After that the section marks the answer **Out of date** and waits for you: age alone never spends a lookup.

## Differences to Look At

Where your data and the register disagree, the section lists it rather than changing anything. Each difference is badged by how much it matters: **Check**, **Review** or **Info**.

- The company is no longer active, for example under liquidation.
- The tax number differs.
- The account you hold is not listed for this company.
- The account belongs to no known bank branch.
- The name or the postal code differs. Addresses are compared on the postal code only, because free-text addresses vary too much to compare honestly.
- The register lists accounts you do not hold, shown as one line with a count.

## What the Register Fills In

When the register knows something you do not, exactly four things are filled in, and only while they are empty: the **address**, the **country**, the full **tax number** where you already hold its first eight digits, and the **VAT group identifier**. The name and the bank accounts are never written by the register. Nothing at all is filled in for a sole trader.

## Accounts Confirmed Automatically

Before an account can go into a payment file, someone has to confirm it: see [Paying Approved Invoices](/paying-approved-invoices). The register can do that in one narrow case, the **first** account ever seen for a supplier, when all of this holds:

- the register lists exactly that account for the supplier's tax number;
- the company is active, and is not a sole trader;
- the register data is less than 90 days old;
- the invoice is an expense invoice that arrived after the check was switched on;
- the account is a 24-digit domestic account number;
- the tax number came from a person, not from a name search.

That last condition is stricter than it sounds: in practice the register confirms an account only for a partner whose tax number someone typed or corrected on the partner page. For everyone else a person still confirms, which is the fraud control working as intended.

**A changed account is never confirmed automatically**, whatever the register says.

The register's answer never blocks an approval or a payment file. What blocks a payment file is an unconfirmed account, and a register confirmation is one way to unblock it.

## Partners Without a Tax Number

A partner with no tax number cannot be checked. Press **Find in the company register** to search by name, which costs nothing, and **Use this tax number** on the right result.

A tax number found by name is marked as such. It fills the gap, but it is **never** used to confirm a bank account automatically, because a name is not proof of identity. If you later type the tax number in yourself, it counts as yours.

## Sole Traders and Private Persons

A sole trader's tax number is sent to the register, and the answer is that they are a private person, so no company data comes back and nothing is filled in. The partner used for private-person sales is never sent at all.

## Who Can See and Use It

Seeing the register's answers needs "View invoices and bank transactions". Pressing **Check now**, **Revalidate** or **Use this tax number** needs "Edit master data". See [Roles and Permissions](/roles-and-permissions).

## Where the Data Goes

Lookups are made at WellData Kft. (cegadatapi.hu), a Hungarian provider in the EU. What is sent is the supplier's tax number, or its name for a name search. What comes back is kept to the company's own registry data: owners, managers, phone numbers, email addresses and branch offices are discarded and never stored. WellData is listed in our sub-processor register.
